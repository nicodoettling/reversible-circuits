# Post-referee resource audit: binary-collision bridge comparisons

## Exact target and conventions

Write `(pq)(x)=p(q(x))`.  Let `E=T_N^2`, let `p:E->A_N` send an
ordered transposition pair to its product, and give `E` its uniform law.  The
target measure is the law of `(A_1,...,A_n) in E^n` conditioned on the
**ordered noncommutative** constraint

```text
p(A_1)p(A_2)...p(A_n)=h.
```

The aligned bridge chain resamples adjacent pairs `(A_i,A_(i+1))` from their
exact conditional law, equivalently preserving the ordered two-increment
product.  The question in this note is whether an existing general
binary-collision theorem directly supplies a gap polynomial in `N,n` for
this chain.

## Closest primary theorems

### Caputo's complete-graph reduction

P. Caputo, "On the spectral gap of the Kac walk and other binary collision
processes," arXiv:0807.3415, Section 2, Theorem 2.1
([primary PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0807.3415)).  Its state space is `X^q`
under `mu^q` conditioned on the additive vector conservation law

```text
sum_(i=1)^q xi(eta_i)=omega,       xi:X->R^d.
```

The key `non-interference property` says that after fixing all coordinates
outside `A`, the coordinates in `A` again have product law conditioned on
the remaining additive sum.  With `F_b` generated by the coordinates
outside an unordered pair `b`, the continuous-time simple-average generator
is normalized as

```text
L f=(1/q) sum_(b subset [q], |b|=2) {nu(f|F_b)-f}.
```

If the three-component gap `lambda(3)` is independent of the conserved
value, Theorem 2.1 gives

```text
lambda(q) >= (3 lambda(3)-1)(1-2/q)+1/q.              (C)
```

This is a complete-graph theorem.  In particular, a positive uniform
consequence requires the three-component constant to exceed `1/3`; it is
not a reduction from an arbitrary positive three-component gap.

### Sasada's local-to-complete comparison

M. Sasada, "On the spectral gap of the Kac walk and other binary collision
processes on d-dimensional lattice," arXiv:1308.5096, Section 2, Theorems
2.1--2.3 ([primary PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1308.5096)).  It uses the same
additively conditioned product measure.  The complete simple-average
generator is

```text
L^* f=(1/q) sum_(1<=i<j<=q) {nu(f|F_(i,j))-f},
```

and on the box `Lambda_L={1,...,L}^d`, of size `q=L^d`, the local generator
is the **unnormalized** nearest-neighbor sum

```text
L^(star,loc) f=sum_(unordered x~y) {nu(f|F_(x,y))-f}.
```

Theorem 2.1 states, for every feasible conserved value `omega`,

```text
lambda^(star,loc)(L,omega)
  >= [1/(96 d L^2)] lambda^*(|Lambda_L|,omega).        (S)
```

Thus in dimension one `(S)` has exactly the attractive diffusive `L^-2`
form.  Theorem 2.3 closes the complete-graph constant only under
`inf_omega lambda^*(3,omega)>1/3` (and, for a nonsimple two-site collision
operator, the additional condition `lambda(2)>0`).

The proof of Sasada's Theorem 2.1 makes exchangeability structural, rather
than cosmetic.  Lemma 1.1 introduces the coordinate swap `pi_(x,z)`, uses
invariance of `nu` under that swap, and uses the exact conjugation identity

```text
E_(x,y) f = pi_(x,z) E_(z,y)(pi_(x,z) f),
```

where `E_(x,y)=nu(.|F_(x,y))`.  Lemma 1.2 then pays for the swap by the
two-coordinate heat-bath Dirichlet form.  These are the steps from which the
canonical-path comparison and the constant `96 d L^2` follow.

## Exact hypothesis mismatch

The ordered-product bridge is not exchangeable.  Here is an explicit
smallest useful witness in `A_4`, written as permutation tuples on
`{0,1,2,3}`:

```text
a=(1,2,0,3) = (0 1 2),
b=(1,3,2,0) = (0 1 3),
c=(2,3,0,1) = (0 2)(1 3).
```

With the convention above, `ab=c`, hence `abc=e`; but swapping the first two
coordinates gives

```text
bac=(1,0,3,2)=(0 1)(2 3) != e.
```

All three increments have positive `Q_4=P_(T_4)^2` mass: `a,b` are
three-cycles and `c` is a double transposition.  Thus even the support of a
fixed-endpoint bridge is not invariant under a raw coordinate swap.  No
constraint of the Caputo--Sasada form `sum_i xi(A_i)=omega` can define this
fiber, because every such level set is permutation invariant.  Consequently
Sasada's `pi_(x,z)` is not an operator on the bridge sector and the displayed
conditional-expectation conjugation identity fails at its first use.

There is a second ordering mismatch.  Caputo's complete dynamics updates
every unordered coordinate pair.  For two nonadjacent bridge coordinates,
preserving their raw two-increment product generally does not preserve the
total ordered product because the intervening increments need not commute.
So even a hypothetical complete-pair bridge chain is not the generator in
`(C)` without introducing conjugated, order-dependent moves.

Finally, the inherited fixed-length estimates such as a positive
`M^{-O(1)}` gap for lengths three or four do not establish the strict
`lambda^*(3)>1/3` input used to turn `(C)` into a size-uniform bound.  That
constant threshold is an independent mismatch.

The microfactor multiplicities themselves are **not** the obstruction:
taking the single-site space to be `E=T_N^2` correctly retains the ordered
pair labels.  The obstruction is the nonabelian ordered conservation law.

## What survives and the first new proposition

For a consecutive interval, fixing its complement leaves a fixed-product
law on the interval after conjugating the required endpoint by the fixed
prefix/suffix.  Centrality of the increment law makes this a useful
braid-covariant analogue of `non-interference`.  It does not supply ordinary
coordinate exchangeability, and neither primary theorem proves the needed
operator comparison.

The smallest standalone extension that would revive this route is:

> **NONCOMMUTATIVE CAPUTO--SASADA COMPARISON.**  For every finite group `G`,
> conjugation-invariant increment law `mu`, feasible endpoint `h`, and
> length `n`, compare the adjacent same-product heat-bath Hamiltonian on the
> ordered bridge `mu^n(. | X_1...X_n=h)` to a quantitatively controlled
> complete/twisted-pair collision Hamiltonian with loss polynomial in `n`
> and no dependence exponential in `|G|` or `1/min mu`.

Any proof must replace literal swaps by Hurwitz/braid shuttles and prove the
conditional-projection and capacity identities that Sasada obtains for free
from exchangeability.  A further bounded-particle theorem would then be
needed for the twisted complete dynamics.

## Verdict

**REFUTED AS A DIRECT THEOREM TRANSFER.**  Caputo Theorem 2.1 and Sasada
Theorems 2.1--2.3 do not apply to the ordered fixed-product bridge.  This is
a precise hypothesis mismatch, not evidence of a slow mode and not a
refutation of polynomial mixing.

No computation beyond the exact displayed `S_4` multiplication is needed;
it can be reproduced by composing the three listed tuples right to left.
