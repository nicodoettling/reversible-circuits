# Cycle 04 M14 l=4 independent referee report

## 1. Claim, quantifiers, and convention

Use `(pq)(x)=p(q(x))`, read a displayed word as the ordered product
`t_1...t_m`, and put `M=binom(N,2)`.  I audited the following frozen
claim.

For every `N>=3` and every feasible pair `u,v in A_N`, with
`h=u^{-1}v`, let the uniform microfactor fiber be

```text
Omega_h^(8)={t in T_N^8:t_1...t_8=h}
```

and let

```text
B_4=(H_{1,4}+H_{3,4}+H_{5,4})/3.
```

Then `B_4` is irreducible, its projection onto four `Q_N=P_N^2`
increments is irreducible, and

```text
gap(B_4;u,v) >= 2/(3M^20),
Lambda_N(4;u,v) = 3 gap(B_4;u,v) >= 2M^-20.
```

For `0<epsilon<1`, the claimed worst-case bound is

```text
tau_B4(epsilon)
 <= (3/2)M^20(8 log M+log(1/epsilon)).
```

A singleton has gap one by convention.

I also audited the later, explicitly nonterminal addendum: for every
`l>=2` and nonempty length-`2l` fiber,

```text
gap(B_l;u,v)
 >= 2/[(l-1)M^4 V(V-1)]
 >= 2/[(l-1)M^(4l+4)],
Lambda_N(l;u,v) >= 2/M^(4l+4),
```

where the first display is for `V=|Omega_h^(2l)|>=2`.

## 2. Full audit

### 2.1 Literature hypothesis and graph quotient

The source numbering is: Douvropoulos--Lewis published Theorem 18 and
Proposition 8(4), respectively Theorem 4.2 and Proposition 2.7(4) in the
section-numbered preprint.  Published Theorem 18 applies to arbitrary-length
reflection factorizations of a parabolic quasi-Coxeter element.

Every `h in S_N` satisfies that hypothesis.  Minimally factoring each
disjoint cycle uses `N-r(h)` transpositions, which is reduced by the usual
cycle-count lower bound, and those factors generate the Young parabolic
product of the symmetric groups on the cycle supports.  In type A, the
subgroup generated by a transposition word is exactly
`prod_C S_C` over the labeled connected components of its factorization
graph.  The subgroup-conjugacy class of a transposition records its particular
component `C`.  The cited criterion therefore becomes exactly:

> Two equal-length transposition words with the same arbitrary product are
> Hurwitz-equivalent if and only if they have the same labeled graph
> components and the same factor count on every component.

Douvropoulos--Lewis use `(a,b)->(b,bab)`; its inverse is the alternate
project convention, so the orbit relation is unchanged.

### 2.2 Component length and square-tree target

Let a nonsingleton connected component `C` contain `v_C` labels and
`q_C` cycles of `h|_C`, counting fixed points, and have `k_C` factors.
Then

```text
k_C=v_C+q_C-2+2g_C,  g_C>=0.
```

Besides the cited proposition, this follows directly.  During multiplication
from the identity, let `J` and `D` be the numbers of cycle joins and cuts.
Then `k_C=J+D` and `q_C=v_C-J+D`.  Every first edge joining two graph
components is a cycle join, so connected support forces `J>=v_C-1`, hence
`D>=q_C-1`; put `g_C=D-(q_C-1)`.

The target is explicit: minimally factor each endpoint cycle
(`v_C-q_C` factors), append adjacent squares on the edges of a tree joining
the `q_C` cycle supports, and append `g_C` adjacent surplus squares.
Its length is the displayed value, its product is `h|_C`, and its graph is
connected.  A fixed endpoint cycle contributes an empty minimal block but is
joined by the tree; an isolated fixed point has weight zero.  The orbit
theorem therefore reaches this target from every word in the macro-fiber.

### 2.3 Alignment and cross-component square replacement

Every adjacent pair among eight positions lies in one of
`[1,4],[3,6],[5,8]`.  More generally, among `2l` positions, the pair
starting at odd `p<=2l-3` lies in the window beginning at `p`, an
even-`p` pair lies in the window beginning at `p-1`, and the last pair
lies in `[2l-3,2l]`.

For transpositions, `b(bab)=ab`.  A Hurwitz replacement on an adjacent pair,
while retaining the other two window factors, is therefore a positive exact
`H_4` outcome.  Similarly, for any transpositions `f,s`,

```text
(f,f) -> (s,s)
```

preserves the four-factor window product, including when the square occupies
the middle two positions or a boundary pair.

This remains valid when `s` lies in a different graph component: the heat
bath is conditioned only on ordered block product, not on graph macro data.
All bridge and surplus squares are exposed simultaneously before
replacement.  Replacing them sequentially cannot destroy adjacency of the
remaining pairs.  A surplus edge might temporarily preserve a connection
after one tree square is removed, but every surplus square is also removed;
after the last replacement no unintended cross-cycle edge remains.

If `h!=id`, choose a fixed nontrivial endpoint cycle `D` and an edge
`s` within it.  Replace every tree-bridge and surplus square by `s^2`.
Every endpoint cycle is then its own component, every fixed point is isolated,
and all

```text
(2l-(N-r(h)))/2
```

excess pairs lie on `D`.  This macro depends only on `h` and `l`.
If `h=id`, the square-tree target consists of exactly `l` adjacent
squares; replacing all of them by one designated `s^2` reaches `s^(2l)`.
If `2l=N-r(h)`, there is no square, but the component formula forces one
component per endpoint cycle and no surplus, so the macro is already unique.
Thus the qualitative proof covers arbitrary endpoint cycle unions, fixed
points, the identity endpoint, and minimum length.

### 2.4 Microfactor/product-path equivalence

Group factors as `A_i=(t_{2i-1},t_{2i})` and
`q_i=prod(A_i) in supp(Q_N)`.  An aligned four-factor update projects to
replacement of two adjacent increments with the same ordered product.
Conversely, every such product replacement lifts by choosing any ordered
two-transposition realization of each new increment.

Above a fixed product path, choose a window containing increment `i`, retain
the exact ordered realization of the neighboring increment, and replace
`A_i` by any other realization with product `q_i`.  This is an exact
same-product four-factor outcome.  Every increment has such a neighboring
window, including both endpoints.  Hence every quotient fiber is connected,
every quotient edge lifts, and the micro and product support graphs have the
same component count.  The projected stationary law is the multiplicity-
weighted push-forward of the uniform micro law; no unjustified uniform
product-path law is used.

This argument uses only `l>=2`, so the lifting and within-quotient-fiber
connectivity statements hold for every `l`, not merely for `l=4`.

### 2.5 Exact code audit

The frozen quotient harness constructs all ordered transposition pairs,
groups them by their product, and retains their exact multiplicities.  For a
path `(a,b,c,d)` it solves

```text
d=c^{-1}b^{-1}a^{-1}h
```

in the declared convention.  Its three union-find keys fix respectively
`(c,d)`, `(a,d)`, and `(a,b)`, exactly the outside increments for the
three aligned windows.  Endpoint partitions with even defect at most eight
are precisely the feasible length-eight conjugacy types; conjugation
equivariance justifies one representative per type.  Multiplying by exact
class sizes and pair multiplicities recovers `M^8`, so endpoint and state
mass are exhaustive.

The harness enumerates the product quotient rather than every micro edge.
That is sufficient only together with the analytic quotient lemma in Section
2.4.  I additionally enumerated the full micro support at `N=3`; it agrees.

### 2.6 Poincare and TV normalization

Let `V=|Omega_h^(8)|`.  A selected window has probability `1/3`, and a
specified block in its exact conditional fiber has probability
`1/F_{4,N}(g)`, where `F_{4,N}(g)<=M^4`.  Every selected positive tree edge
therefore has transition probability at least

```text
p_*=1/(3M^4).
```

Choose a spanning tree of the connected transition graph.  For uniform
`pi=1/V`,

```text
Var_pi(f)
 <= (V-1)/2 sum_tree (nabla f)^2,
E_B4(f,f)
 >= p_*/V sum_tree (nabla f)^2.
```

The first inequality follows by routing every ordered pair along its unique
tree path: path length is at most `V-1` and an edge is used by at most
`V^2` ordered pairs.  Thus

```text
gap(B_4)>=2p_*/[V(V-1)].
```

Since `V<=M^8`, this is at least `2/(3M^20)`.  The identity
`mathcal H_4=3(I-B_4)` gives `Lambda_N(4;u,v)>=2M^-20`; no factor is
missing.

Each heat bath is an orthogonal conditional-expectation projection in the
uniform fiber law.  Their average is therefore self-adjoint and positive
semidefinite even though the projections need not commute.  Consequently the
ordinary spectral gap controls the absolute nontrivial spectrum.
Since `pi_min=1/V>=M^-8`, the standard worst-case TV estimate gives the
displayed bound.  Its logarithmic term is deliberately looser than the
`(1/2)log(1/pi_min)` estimate, so the usual integer-time ceiling is absorbed
for `N>=3`.

### 2.7 All-length addendum

For `B_l=(l-1)^{-1}sum_{j=1}^{l-1}H_{2j-1,4}`, the same alignment and macro
proof applies for every `l>=2`.  Now

```text
p_*>=1/[(l-1)M^4],  V<=M^(2l).
```

The identical tree calculation gives

```text
gap(B_l)>=2/[(l-1)M^4V(V-1)]
         >=2/[(l-1)M^(4l+4)].
```

Since
`mathcal H_l=(l-1)(I-B_l)`, its gap is at least
`2/M^(4l+4)`.  At `l=2` the true chain is the whole-word heat bath with
gap one, so the weaker display remains valid.  The addendum's TV bound follows
from `pi_min>=M^(-2l)` by the same PSD argument.

When `V=1`, the expression containing `V(V-1)` is not invoked: the state
space is a singleton and its gap is one by convention.  The second displayed
bound remains valid for `N>=3,l>=2` because its right-hand side is below one.
The Hamiltonian inequality is likewise vacuous on the empty nonconstant
spectrum (or valid under the compatible singleton convention); the spectral
identity is used quantitatively only when `V>=2`.

This extension is mathematically valid but nonterminal: its exponent depends
linearly on `l`, its tree construction may enumerate `M^(2l)` states, and
it is superpolynomial in the requested `l=Theta(N log N)` regime.

## 3. Probability, runtime, path, and congestion accounting

- At `l=4`, scan probability is exactly `1/3`; at general `l`, it is
  exactly `1/(l-1)`.  Conditional block probability is exactly
  `1/F_{4,N}(g)>=M^-4`.
- At `l=4`, `V<=M^8`, tree path length is at most `V-1<=M^8-1`,
  ordered-pair edge load is at most `V^2<=M^16`, and stationary directed
  capacity is at least `p_*/V`.  These yield exactly the exponent 20 and
  coefficient `2/3`.
- At general `l`, the corresponding bounds are `V<=M^(2l)`, path length
  `V-1`, and load `V^2<=M^(4l)`, yielding exponent `4l+4`.
- One exact update can enumerate at most `M^4` candidate blocks.  A full
  l=4 transition tree can be constructed by enumerating at most `M^8`
  states, polynomial in `N` because four is fixed.  The analogous
  `M^(2l)` construction is not polynomial jointly in `N,l`.

## 6. Strongest self-attack

The strongest possible failure point is the imported orbit classification:
without its arbitrary-length conclusion or with a mismatched generated
subgroup, the square-tree target would establish existence but not reachability.
The primary-source audit checks precisely those hypotheses, and the direct
type-A reduction above finds no mismatch.

The canonical macro is a connectivity device, not a scalable flow.  It can
have exponentially small stationary mass when `l` grows.  The quantitative
proof avoids routing through it only by taking an arbitrary spanning tree of
the entire fixed-length state graph; its `V^2` load is polynomial solely
because `l=4` is fixed.  Thus the proof has no hidden route to the terminal
polynomial theorem.  The finite checks cannot repair a defect in the
classification, but they expose no counterexample.
