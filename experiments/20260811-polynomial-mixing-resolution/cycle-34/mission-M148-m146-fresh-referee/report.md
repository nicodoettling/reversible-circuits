## Field 3 — centralizer parity criterion

For `p in S_d`, `C_(S_d)(p)` is wholly even exactly when the cycle type has
distinct odd parts. An even cycle is itself an odd centralizer element. Two
equal odd cycles can be interchanged pointwise by a product of an odd number
of transpositions; this includes repeated fixed points. If all parts are odd
and distinct, no cycles can be permuted and the centralizer is the direct
product of their cyclic groups, all generated by even permutations. Thus both
directions and every repeated-cycle case are covered. Clean-room enumeration
of every cycle type for `2<=d<=6` found zero failures.

## Field 4 — product centralizers and class splitting

Let `G=S_d^s`, let `sigma:G->F_2^s` record coordinate parity, and let
`P=sigma^(-1)(Delta)` for the diagonal subspace. For `x in P`, the parity
image `B` of `C_G(x)=product_q C_(S_d)(x_q)` is exactly the coordinate
subspace supported on nonrigid coordinates. Since `P` is normal, the
`P`-classes inside the ambient `G`-class are the double cosets

```text
P \ G / C_G(x),
```

whose count is `[G:P C_G(x)]=[F_2^s:Delta+B]`. If `r>=1` coordinates are
rigid, `B` has dimension `s-r` and `Delta` adds one dimension, giving
`2^(r-1)`. If `r=0`, then `B=F_2^s` and the count is one. This is precisely
`2^max(r-1,0)`.

Likewise, transporter parity is fixed at rigid coordinates and independently
flippable at nonrigid ones. A common-parity transporter exists exactly when
the fixed rigid-coordinate parities agree; this is vacuous for `r<=1`.
Direct orbit enumeration at `(d,s)=(2,1..3),(3,1..3),(4,1..2)` found zero
formula failures.

## Field 5 — all-odd and transposition tuples

An odd coordinate element is itself an odd member of its centralizer. Hence
an all-odd tuple has `r=0`, and its ambient product class does not split. All
transpositions are coordinatewise conjugate in `S_d^s`, so all `s`-tuples of
transpositions form one `P_(d,s)` class for every `d>=2,s>=1`.

The explicit transporter argument is also valid: multiply an odd coordinate
transporter by the source transposition, which centralizes the source and
reverses transporter parity. Thus all coordinate transporters may be chosen
even. Every bounded direct-orbit row independently found exactly one
transposition-tuple class.

## Field 6 — Hurwitz necessity and M141/M143 application

A common signed right-Hurwitz move replaces a neighboring aligned factor pair
by one old factor and a conjugate of the other inside the literal generated
group. It preserves that group and the multiset of factor conjugacy classes.
This is necessary, not sufficient, for a common braid.

For every nonempty `d>=5` M135 matching subfamily, accepted M141/M143 gives
the literal equalities `Gamma_U=Gamma_V=P_(d,s)`. Every source and target
factor is an `s`-tuple of transpositions, hence belongs to the single class
from Field 5. Both sequences have `2d-2` factors, so both multisets consist of
`2d-2` copies of that class. The use of literal group equality, not abstract
isomorphism, is explicit and correct.
