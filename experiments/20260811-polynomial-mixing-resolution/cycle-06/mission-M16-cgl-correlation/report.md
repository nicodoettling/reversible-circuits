## 1. Exact claim, quantifiers, and convention

Permutations compose as (pq)(x)=p(q(x)), and tuple products are read from
left to right.  Let N>=3, T_N be the transpositions, E=T_N^2, and
p(a,b)=ab.  For l>=2 and every feasible h in A_N, let pi_(l,h) be uniform on

    Omega_l(h)={x in E^l:p(x_1)...p(x_l)=h}.

For i<j, P_ij is conditional expectation given every raw microcoordinate
outside {i,j}, and

    C_(l,h)=binom(l,2)^(-1) sum_(i<j) P_ij.

Let R_k be conditional expectation given x_k and
A_(l,h)=l^(-1)sum_k R_k.  Gaps are discrete-time Poincare gaps; a singleton
fiber has gap one.  Let mu_l(h) be the top mean-zero eigenvalue of A_(l,h).

The following scoped claims are proved.

1. For every l>=3 and every feasible h,

       gap(C_(l,h))
       >= min_q gap(C_(l-1,q)) [1-mu_l(h)],             (1.1)

   where q ranges over feasible shorter endpoints.  The deletion
   intertwining includes every pair straddling the deleted coordinate.

2. The nonzero mean-zero spectrum of A_(l,h) is exactly an l-by-l block Gram
   spectrum.  Its common forward correlation block K_(l,h) is normal, and
   every block reduces to an explicit l-by-l scalar Hermitian Toeplitz
   matrix.  The only nontrivial one-coordinate sector has dimension
   O(N^4).

3. The newly frozen dense-tail lemma in
   cycle-05/mission-M10-parent-hamiltonian/cgl_complete_chain_checkpoint.md,
   Section 3.2, is accepted: there is a universal C_mix such that for every
   feasible h and l>=C_mix N log(N+1),

       ||K_(l,h)||_(H_0 to H_0) <= 1/(4l),
       mu_l(h) < 5/(4l).                                (1.2)

4. The literal accumulated product beginning at l=2 is false because
   mu_2^*=1.  The stronger candidate mu_l<=1/(l-1) for l>=3 is also
   rigorously false.  The broader claim mu_l<=A/l for some universal A is
   neither proved nor refuted below the dense threshold.

Dense-tail referee verdict: ACCEPT.  Numbered objections: none.

These are nonterminal results.  They do not prove a base-scale gap through
l=O(N log N), Terminal A, or Terminal B.

## 2. Full proof and construction

### 2.1 Braid deletion, including crossing pairs

For a microstate x=(a,b) and sigma in S_N, write

    x^sigma=(sigma^(-1)a sigma,sigma^(-1)b sigma).

The right micro-Hurwitz bijection is

    H(x,y)=(y,x^(p(y))).

If g=p(y), then the new product is
g(g^(-1)p(x)g)=p(x)g, so H preserves the ordered product and uniform
counting measure.

Fix x_k=z and g=p(z).  Apply H successively at positions
(k-1,k),(k-2,k-1),..., (1,2).  The resulting word is

    (z,x_1^g,...,x_(k-1)^g,x_(k+1),...,x_l).

Deleting its first coordinate leaves a bridge of endpoint g^(-1)h.  On the
fixed-z slice this is a product of one-coordinate bijections: conjugation by
g for coordinates before k and the identity for coordinates after k.
It therefore maps the sigma-field generated by the complement of every
pair avoiding k onto the complement sigma-field of the corresponding
shorter pair.  This statement includes i<k<j.  Conditional expectations are
covariant under measure-preserving bijections, so every such P_ij is
intertwined with the corresponding shorter-bridge heat bath.

Moving z to the right boundary with the inverse convention gives endpoint
h g^(-1).  The two endpoints are conjugate, but the proof fixes the
left-shuttle convention g^(-1)h and does not interchange the formulas.

Define

    Q_l^(k)=binom(l-1,2)^(-1)
            sum_(i<j; i,j != k) P_ij.

Every raw pair occurs in exactly l-2 leave-one-out sums, and hence

    C_(l,h)=l^(-1)sum_k Q_l^(k).                        (2.1)

Let delta be the worst shorter-fiber gap.  On every x_k slice, apply the
shorter Poincare inequality to f-R_k f:

    <f,Q_l^(k)f>
    <=(1-delta)||f||^2+delta <f,R_k f>.

Average in k, use (2.1), and take the supremum on mean-zero functions.  This
proves (1.1).  No commutation between crossing P_ij is used.

There is also a useful exact comparison between the two auxiliary
operators.  If k is outside {i,j}, then sigma(x_k) is contained in the
sigma-field outside {i,j}.  Thus range(R_k) is contained in range(P_ij) and

    I-R_k >= I-P_ij

as quadratic forms.  Sum over all triples (k,{i,j}) with k outside the pair.
Each k is repeated binom(l-1,2) times and each pair l-2 times.  After the
normalizations cancel,

    I-A_(l,h) >= I-C_(l,h),                             (2.2)

so 1-mu_l(h)=gap(A_(l,h))>=gap(C_(l,h)).  At l=3 there is equality because
each pair complement is a single coordinate.

### 2.2 Exact Gram spectrum and central marginal formulas

Let rho be the common one-coordinate marginal, H_k=L^2_0(E,rho), and
J_k u=u(x_k).  Define

    T(u_1,...,u_l)=l^(-1/2)sum_k J_k u_k.

Then A_(l,h)=TT*, so its nonzero mean-zero spectrum is exactly that of
G=T*T.  Its blocks are

    G_ij=l^(-1)J_i*J_j,

the coordinate conditional-correlation operators.

Put

    W=(sum_(t in T_N)t)^2=sum_(x in E)p(x),
    H_r(q)=[q]W^r.

The element W is central.  For a fixed microstate x with g=p(x),

    Pr(X_i=x)=H_(l-1)(g^(-1)h)/H_l(h),                 (2.3)

independently of i.  For i<j and fixed x,y with products g,s,

    Pr(X_i=x,X_j=y)
      =H_(l-2)((gs)^(-1)h)/H_l(h),                     (2.4)

independently of j-i.  Centrality permits every intervening convolution
power to pass the two fixed increments while retaining their order.

Consequently G has I/l on the diagonal, K/l strictly above the diagonal,
and K*/l strictly below, where

    (Kf)(x)=sum_y
       H_(l-2)((p(x)p(y))^(-1)h)
       /H_(l-1)(p(x)^(-1)h) f(y).                      (2.5)

The endpoint law depends on a microstate only through p(x).  Functions with
zero mean inside every product fiber are killed by both off-diagonal
blocks and contribute the exact eigenvalue 1/l.  The remaining product
support is

    {e, 3-cycles, double transpositions},

of size 1+2 binom(N,3)+3 binom(N,4)=O(N^4).

### 2.3 Cyclic covariance, normality, and scalar diagonalization

Define theta(x)=h^(-1)x h componentwise and Uf=f composed with theta.  The
bridge map

    (x_1,...,x_l) -> (x_2,...,x_l,theta(x_1))           (2.6)

preserves the endpoint and uniform measure.  If g_1...g_l=h, its product is

    g_2...g_l(h^(-1)g_1h)=g_1^(-1)h h^(-1)g_1h=h.

All ordered location pairs have the same law by (2.4).  Applying (2.6)
l-1 times therefore gives

    (X,Y) has the same law as (Y,theta(X)).              (2.7)

The marginal is theta-invariant, so U is unitary.  For the complex L^2
inner product, (2.7) yields

    <f,Kg>=<Kf,Ug>,   hence K=K*U.                      (2.8)

Taking adjoints and comparing gives

    K*=U^(-1)K=KU^(-1),   KU=UK.

Thus K is normal.  It and U have a simultaneous orthonormal eigenbasis.
If Kv=kappa v and Uv=u v, then

    kappa=conjugate(kappa)u.                            (2.9)

For this one-coordinate eigenvector, the associated l-by-l Gram block is
1/l times the Hermitian matrix with diagonal 1, every strict upper entry
kappa, and every strict lower entry conjugate(kappa).

Write kappa=r exp(i phi).  If kappa is nonreal, put

    theta_n=(-2phi+2pi n)/l,   n=0,...,l-1.

The scalar Gram eigenvalues are

    (1/l)[1-r sin(phi+theta_n/2)/sin(theta_n/2)].       (2.10)

Equivalently, the geometric eigenvector ratio q satisfies
q^l=conjugate(kappa)/kappa.  If kappa is real, the eigenvalues are

    [1+(l-1)kappa]/l                    once,
    (1-kappa)/l                         multiplicity l-1.  (2.11)

For kappa=0 all are 1/l.  Direct arbitrary-noncentral-endpoint calculations
through N=8 verify (2.8)-(2.11) to floating error below 10^(-14), but the
proof above is universal and does not depend on those calculations.

### 2.4 Exact failures and exceptional-mode replacement

At l=2, the product of one coordinate determines the product of the other.
There is a nonconstant one-coordinate function with eigenvalue one, so
mu_2^*=1.  Any recursion product must begin at l=3.

At l=3, C_3=A_3.  The inherited exact N=6 endpoint of cycle type
(2,2,1,1) has a mean-zero Rayleigh certificate with

    gap(C_3)
    <=11308644378456917647/39796565095659282750
    <1/3.

Thus mu_3>2/3>1/2, rigorously refuting mu_l<=1/(l-1).

A refined CGL statement survives.  Fix eta<mu_l.  Let E_eta be the sum of
the mean-zero A_l eigenspaces above eta, V_eta the smallest C_l-invariant
subspace containing E_eta, and nu_eta the top C_l eigenvalue on V_eta.
Because V_eta reduces the self-adjoint C_l, either

    lambda_2(C_l)<=nu_eta,

or every competing eigenvector lies in V_eta perpendicular.  On that
orthogonal complement the A_l Rayleigh quotient is at most eta.  Applying
the same quadratic inequality used in (1.1) gives

    gap(C_l)>=gap_worst(C_(l-1))(1-eta).                (2.12)

The exceptional additive modes are explicit.  For every eigenpair
Kv=kappa v and every scalar eigenvector ratio q in (2.10)-(2.11), they are
spanned by functions of the form

    F_(kappa,q)(X)=sum_(j=1)^l q^(j-1)v(X_j)

whose scalar eigenvalue exceeds eta, together with the 1/l microfiber modes
when 1/l>eta.  What is not known is a polynomial C_l gap on the
C_l-invariant closure of these modes below the dense threshold.

### 2.5 Independent audit of the dense-tail lemma

Let P_T be the nonlazy uniform-transposition walk on S_N.  One microincrement
is two independent transpositions, so its product law is exactly

    Q=P_T^2,   Q^s=P_T^(2s).                            (2.13)

This is the required parity/time conversion: Q lives on A_N and one Q step
is two, not one, transposition steps.

The accepted parity-aware Diaconis--Shahshahani pointwise audit gives a
universal A and an integer s_0(N)<=A N log(N+1) for which the positive
Fourier majorant R_(s_0) is at most 1/16.  After removing the trivial and
sign representations, every transposition character ratio obeys

    |r_lambda|<=rho_N:=1-2/(N-1)

for N>=4; at N=3 the only remaining ratio is zero.  Hence for s>=s_0,

    max_g ||A_N|Q^s(g)-1|
    <=R_s
    <=(1/16)rho_N^(2(s-s_0))
    <=(1/16)exp[-4(s-s_0)/(N-1)].                      (2.14)

Choose one universal C_mix>A large enough.  At
l=C_mix N log(N+1), the last exponential with s=l-2 is at most 1/(4l).
For larger l, l exp[-4(l-2-s_0)/(N-1)] is decreasing.  The finitely many
small N are absorbed by enlarging C_mix.  Thus, simultaneously for
s in {l-2,l-1,l},

    epsilon_s:=max_g ||A_N|Q^s(g)-1|<=1/(64l).          (2.15)

No additional log l time is needed: beyond the N log N threshold the
exponential in l/N uniformly dominates 1/l.

Let nu be the law of (X_i,X_j), and mu the common marginal.  Substitute

    |A_N|Q^s(a)=1+delta_s(a),   |delta_s(a)|<=epsilon

into (2.3)-(2.4).  The two-coordinate density relative to mu tensor mu is
the product of two numerator factors divided by two denominator factors,
each in [1-epsilon,1+epsilon].  Therefore

    ((1-epsilon)/(1+epsilon))^2
    <=dnu/d(mu tensor mu)
    <=((1+epsilon)/(1-epsilon))^2,

and the sharp larger deviation is

    ||dnu/d(mu tensor mu)-1||_infinity
    <=4epsilon/(1-epsilon)^2
    <=16epsilon=1/(4l).                                (2.16)

For centered u,v, subtract the product law from their covariance and apply
Cauchy-Schwarz under mu tensor mu.  This proves

    ||K_(l,h)||_(H_0 to H_0)<=1/(4l)                   (2.17)

uniformly in h.

Finally, for a=(||u_1||,...,||u_l||),

    2 sum_(i<j)a_i a_j <=(l-1)sum_i a_i^2.

Using (2.17) in the exact Gram form gives

    <u,Gu>
    <=[1+(l-1)/(4l)]/l ||u||^2
    <5/(4l)||u||^2.                                    (2.18)

This proves the frozen dense-tail claim with all constants checked.  The
argument does not estimate gap(C_(L_0,h)) at
L_0=ceil(C_mix N log(N+1)).

### 2.6 Exact identity-endpoint phase-mode tests

The strong universal O(1/l) claim was attacked with two analytically
specified identity-endpoint statistics, not just full finite spectra.

Let c_lambda be the content sum and d_lambda the Specht dimension.  Since
the transposition class sum acts by c_lambda,

    N! H_r(e)=sum_(lambda partition N)
                 d_lambda^2 c_lambda^(2r).             (2.19)

For Y_i=1{p(X_i)=e}, exact ratios of (2.19) give its pair correlation.

For the stronger fixed-label statistic Z_i=1{p(X_i) moves label 0}, let A be
the transposition class sum of the stabilizer S_(N-1).  The weighted
group-algebra element for Z=1 is exactly

    F=W-A^2-(N-1)e.                                    (2.20)

Indeed, a product of two transpositions fixes 0 precisely when both avoid 0,
or when both equal the same transposition (0 a).  Under the
multiplicity-free branching S_N down S_(N-1), on the mu summand inside
lambda, F has eigenvalue

    c_lambda^2-c_mu^2-(N-1).                            (2.21)

Thus traces of F and F^2 give exact integer formulas for
[e]FW^(l-1) and [e]F^2W^(l-2), hence the exact Z pair correlation.

The exact partition calculation through N=40 did not find a constant
correlation obstruction.  Representative values of l times correlation
(square, label) are:

    (N,l)=(20,10): (0.05896,1.15195)
    (N,l)=(30,15): (0.03503,1.25230)
    (N,l)=(40,20): (0.02465,1.34016)
    (N,l)=(20,20): (0.01386,0.64160)
    (N,l)=(30,30): (0.00790,0.80441)
    (N,l)=(40,40): (0.00527,0.92717)
    (N,l)=(40,149): (5.51e-7,1.05e-4).

Separately, maximizing the full Gram eigenvalue over every endpoint
conjugacy type in the feasible bounded grids gives:

    (N,l,l mu_l^*)=(6,3,2.14752), (7,3,2.50573), (8,3,2.50388),
    (N,l,l mu_l^*)=(7,4,2.07968), (8,4,2.39430).

Thus the coefficient-one proposal fails, while the computed values do not
refute mu_l<=A/l for a larger universal A.  This is finite evidence only.

These exact finite values are consistent with, but do not prove, O(1/l).
They specifically reject these two latent identity-support modes as an
observed constant-correlation obstruction on the tested grid.  Fifteen
overlapping values through N=8 agree with independent full-group enumeration
to at most 9.5e-16.

## 3. Probability, runtime, path, and congestion accounting

- C_(l,h) chooses each of binom(l,2) raw pairs with coefficient
  1/binom(l,2); the leave-one-out identity (2.1) has the exact factor 1/l.
- A_(l,h) chooses each single-coordinate sigma-field with coefficient 1/l.
- The braid deletion is a deterministic measure-preserving bijection of
  length at most l-1.  It is used only for exact conjugacy of conditional
  kernels, not as a comparison flow, so there is no path congestion.
- The dense pointwise error is 1/(64l).  Four density factors give at most
  4epsilon/(1-epsilon)^2<=1/(4l), and the l-by-l Gram sum gives
  mu_l<5/(4l).
- The finite group harness uses exact integer convolution counts followed by
  deterministic floating diagonalization of an O(lN^4) product Gram matrix.
  It has no random seed.  The scalable identity-mode harness uses p(N)
  partitions and exact big-integer character/branching formulas; the full
  N<=40 grid ran in approximately 11 seconds in Python 3.12.3.
- No canonical paths, randomized routing, or congestion estimates enter the
  proved recursion or dense-tail theorem.

## 5. First unproved standalone proposition

BASE-SCALE CGL ACCUMULATION.  There are universal c>0 and C<infinity such
that, for every N>=3 and

    L_0=ceil(C_mix N log(N+1)),

the worst feasible endpoints satisfy

    product_(l=3)^L_0 [1-mu_l^*] >= c (N L_0)^(-C),     (5.1)

where mu_l^*=max_h mu_l(h).

An equally sufficient replacement is: for a threshold eta_l=O(1/l), the
complete-pair chain C_(l,h) has a universal inverse-polynomial gap on the
C_l-invariant closure of the explicit exceptional scalar modes from
Section 2.4, uniformly for 3<=l<=L_0.  The dense-tail theorem handles every
larger l.

## 6. Strongest self-attack

1. The dense-tail ACCEPT result starts only at C_mix N log(N+1).  Iterating
   (1.1) from l=3 to that scale may still lose superpolynomially.
2. The exact scalar diagonalization reduces the correlation problem but does
   not bound the eigenvalues kappa below the smoothing threshold.
3. The identity square and fixed-label tests probe only two explicit
   one-coordinate directions.  The maximal correlation may lie in a
   nonclass, non-label-incidence, or arbitrary-endpoint sector.
4. The invariant closure V_eta can be much larger than its polynomial
   dimensional additive seed E_eta.  Its definition alone supplies no gap.
5. The dense amplification uses the accepted character majorant at one
   fixed-error time.  It proves existence of a universal C_mix, not an
   optimized numerical value.
6. None of these results gives a literal bottleneck or a full-K slow mode.
   Failure of an auxiliary correlation bound would not by itself be
   Terminal B.
7. The local-to-complete comparison and the long-bridge recursion remain
   separate dependencies in any eventual Terminal-A synthesis.

## 7. Calibration results

- N=2 gives singleton behavior and is outside the stated N>=3 theorem.
- l=1 corresponds to original m=2 and has no pair update; it is excluded.
- l=2 corresponds to original m=4.  C_2=P_12 is the whole-word H_4 heat
  bath, while A_2 has mu_2=1.  This explains why the CGL recursion begins at
  l=3 without discarding the original H_4 term.
- At N=3, Q is already uniform on A_3 after one pair step; the exact identity
  square and label correlations vanish.  Singleton shorter fibers obey the
  stated gap-one convention.
- N=4 arbitrary noncentral endpoints test that K need not be self-adjoint
  while K=K*U and normality still hold.  The known A_4 crossing-projection
  witness does not affect the deletion proof.
- The exact N=6 type-(2,2,1,1) witness refutes the 1/(l-1) candidate.
- The grouped microbridge retains product-fiber multiplicities and the
  original counts S_3,m=4: 27; S_4,m=4: 120; S_4,m=6: 3936.
- Odd original m is empty; the grouped model always has original m=2l.
  Micro length l itself may be odd or even.
- Identity and nonidentity endpoints, rare product classes, and every pair
  straddling a deleted coordinate are included.
- No edge-parity summary, fixed canonical hub, L=2 simulation assertion, or
  finite convergence inference is used.
