# Adjacent-Pair Heat-Bath Reduction

## Definition

On the same state space and uniform law, define

    J_{N,m} = (1/2) I
      + (1/(2(m-1))) sum_{j=1}^{m-1} H_{j,2},

where `H_{j,2}` resamples positions `j,j+1` uniformly conditional on their
ordered product. This is an auxiliary chain, not a replacement target.

Let `M=binom(N,2)` and let `F_r(h)` count length-`r` transposition words with
product `h` in the run-manifest convention.

## Proposition

For every `N>=3`, even `m>=4`, and every real function `f` on `Omega(N,m)`,

    E_K(f,f) >= (1/(3 M^2)) E_J(f,f).

Consequently,

    gap(K_{N,m}) >= (1/(3 M^2)) gap(J_{N,m}).

Thus an inverse-polynomial gap for `J` in `N,m` is sufficient for TERMINAL A
once combined with the audited irreducibility theorem and sampler cost.

## Proof

Consider an off-diagonal `J` transition `x->y` generated by resampling the
pair at positions `j,j+1`. The old and new pairs have the same product `p`.
If `j<=m-2`, pad on the right by the unchanged factor at `j+2` and choose the
length-three window beginning at `j`. If `j=m-1`, pad on the left and choose
the window beginning at `m-2`. In either case the exact `H_3` conditional
fiber contains the proposed padded new triple.

For the selected padding, write `h` for the triple product. The point
transition probabilities contributed by these scans are

    K(x,y) >= 1/[2 |R_m| (m-2) F_3(h)],
    J(x,y)  = 1/[2 (m-1) F_2(p)].

For every group element `g`, conditioning on the last transposition gives

    F_3(g) = sum_{c in T_N} F_2(g c).

Moreover `F_2(u)<=M` for every `u`, because choosing the first transposition
determines the second. Hence `F_3(g)<=M^2`. Since the old pair witnesses
`F_2(p)>=1`, and `|R_m|<=3`,

    K(x,y)/J(x,y)
      >= [(m-1)/(|R_m|(m-2))] [F_2(p)/F_3(h)]
      >= 1/(3M^2).

The same uniform stationary law weights both Dirichlet forms. Summing the
pointwise bound over off-diagonal ordered pairs proves the form comparison,
and taking the Poincare infimum proves the gap comparison. QED.

## Exact path-space interpretation

For `w=(t_1,...,t_m)`, set `x_0=id` and `x_k=t_1...t_k`. Then
`x_0=x_m=id`, and consecutive `x` values are adjacent in the undirected
random-transposition Cayley graph. Conversely every such rooted length-`m`
loop gives one word. Uniform word measure is uniform path-bridge measure.

Updating factors `j,j+1` while holding their product fixed is exactly
resampling the single internal prefix vertex `x_j` uniformly among common
neighbors of `x_{j-1}` and `x_{j+1}`. Therefore `J` is lazy random-scan
single-site heat bath for fixed-endpoint path homomorphisms into the
random-transposition Cayley graph, restricted to the forced parity class.

The two-step conditional fiber has a particularly explicit form:

- endpoint displacement `id`: `F_2=M`, the square choices `(t,t)`;
- endpoint displacement a 3-cycle: `F_2=3`;
- endpoint displacement two disjoint transpositions: `F_2=2`;
- all other displacements: infeasible.

This exposes the quantitative question without solving it: prove a polynomial
Poincare inequality for this growing-target, bipartite, hard-constraint path
Glauber chain. Existing fixed-`H` path-colouring bounds cannot be imported if
their constants grow exponentially in `|H|=N!` or require blocks longer than
the available local dynamics.

## Directionality warning

The comparison is one-way. A polynomial lower bound for `gap(J)` proves one
for `K`. A slow mode or bottleneck for `J` does not refute rapid mixing of the
larger chain `K`.
