The result covers **every identity circuit over the specified gate set at one length**, \(m=4r\). The main limitation is that \(4r\) is a special length: it is the **shortest length not divisible by three** at which these gates can compute the identity. That forces every circuit in the fiber to have a noncrossing structure. At your preferred length \(2N-2\), that structure disappears.

## Which identity circuits are covered?

Recall the parameters
\[
N=3r+1,\qquad 3\nmid r,\qquad
\rho=(2\,3\,4)(5\,6\,7)\cdots(N-2\,\,N-1\,\,N),
\qquad g_a=(1\,a)\rho.
\]
The alphabet is exactly \(A_N=\{g_2,\ldots,g_N\}\). The walk chooses five consecutive gates and allows a replacement with the same product.

Take **\(N=7\)**. Then \(r=2\), \(\rho=(2\,3\,4)(5\,6\,7)\), and the proved length is **eight**. There are exactly 72 length-eight identity words over these six gates, and the result covers all 72. The count follows from the minimal star-factorization formula. genui{"citation":{"ref":"turn531359385view0"}}

| Identity circuit | Length | What the result says |
|---|---:|---|
| \(g_2g_7g_5g_6g_7g_2g_3g_4\) | 8 | Covered. The walk can start here and mix over all 72 words. |
| \((g_2g_4)^3\) | 6 | Outside the proved length, even though it uses the same gates. Here \(g_2g_4=\rho^2\). |
| \(g_2^{12}\) | 12 | Outside the proved length. This is exactly \(2N-2\). Indeed, \(g_2=(1\,2\,3\,4)(5\,6\,7)\) has order 12. |
| \(e^8\) | 8 | Requires adding an idle gate. In that enlarged alphabet this circuit is **isolated** under five-gate updates. |

## Why length \(4r\) is special

For a word \(g_{a_1}\cdots g_{a_L}\), move all copies of \(\rho\) to the right. This gives
\[
g_{a_1}\cdots g_{a_L}
=t_1\cdots t_L\,\rho^L,
\qquad
t_j=(1\,\,\rho^{j-1}(a_j)).
\]
This is a position-by-position bijection. Thus a length-\(L\) identity word corresponds exactly to a length-\(L\) factorization of \(\rho^{-L}\) into ordinary star transpositions.

- **If \(3\mid L\)**, the target is \(e\). Every even such length is possible, since the normalized word can consist of cancelling pairs.
- **If \(3\nmid L\)**, the target consists of \(r\) disjoint three-cycles. Its minimum star-factorization length is exactly \(4r\). Every even length above that minimum is also possible by inserting cancelling pairs into the normalized word. genui{"citation":{"ref":"turn531359385view0"}}

At \(L=4r\), minimality forces **each triple of \(\rho\) to occur exactly four times** among the gate indices. Moreover, two different triples cannot occur in the order \(A,B,A,B\), even as a subsequence. One triple can be nested inside another. For example, the covered eight-gate word in the table has triple pattern \(A\,B\,B\,B\,B\,A\,A\,A\). Irving and Rattan give the precise classification, including the allowed order within each triple. genui{"citation":{"ref":"turn437930070view1"}}

A consequence is that **no covered circuit contains a proper nonempty contiguous identity subcircuit**. If it did, deleting that subcircuit would split \(4r\) into two shorter positive identity lengths. Both would have to be multiples of six, whereas \(4r\) is not divisible by three.

For your project, the important substitution is
\[
L=2N-2=6r
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\rho^{-L}=e.
\]
At that length the full fiber is exactly equivalent, under the position-by-position relabeling, to **all ordinary star-transposition identity words of length \(6r\)**. This includes words that repeatedly use a few star labels. The minimal-factorization argument gives no conductance bound for that full fiber.

## Why adding an idle gate or inverses matters

Adding \(e\) already breaks five-gate mixing for every admissible \(r\ge2\). Starting from \(e^{4r}\), a move would have to create a positive identity word using at most five nonidle gates. The shortest such word has length six. So \(e^{4r}\) is isolated while the original identity circuits are still present. The enlarged full fiber has **zero conductance**.

Adding inverses gives a stronger obstruction. Assign \(+1\) to each \(g_a\) and \(-1\) to each \(g_a^{-1}\), and let \(d(w)\) be their sum modulo three. **Every product-preserving rewrite of fewer than \(2r\) consecutive gates preserves \(d(w)\).** To see why, normalize the two windows as above. If their counts differed modulo three, their equal products would express \(\rho\) or \(\rho^{-1}\) using fewer than \(4r\) star transpositions, contradicting the minimum length.

Consequently, for admissible \(r\ge4\), these two kinds of length-\(4r\) identity circuits lie in **different components** of the five-gate walk:

- Every identity from the original positive alphabet has \(d=4r\bmod 3\ne0\).
- The circuit \((g_ag_a^{-1})^{2r}\) has \(d=0\).

So the existence result relies on three precise choices: the prescribed length \(4r\), the alphabet containing only the \(g_a\), and locality measured in consecutive **gate positions**. Each \(g_a\) itself moves all \(N\) points. If your requirement includes an inverse-closed gate set or freely available idle gates, this particular construction fails on the full fiber.
