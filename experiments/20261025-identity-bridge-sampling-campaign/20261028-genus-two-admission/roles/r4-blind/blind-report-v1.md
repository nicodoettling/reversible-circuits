## Common conventions and elementary facts

All labels and positions are distinguished. Products act rightmost first. For a word with product `sigma` and final transposition `t`, the prefix has product `sigma t`. The subgroup generated by a set of transpositions is transitive on its labels precisely when its support graph is connected: graph paths generate all transpositions within each component.

A connected transposition factorization of a permutation on `n` labels with `c` cycles has length `n+c-2+2g` for an integer `g>=0`. Here is a purely combinatorial justification of nonnegativity. Starting at the identity and adding transpositions, let `J` be the number joining two current permutation cycles and `K` the number splitting one. Then `c=n-J+K` and `r=J+K`. At least `n-1` operations join two previously distinct support components; each is a permutation-cycle join. Thus `J>=n-1`, and `r-(n+c-2)=2(J-n+1)>=0`.

This argument also handles proper subsets of labels in recursive calls. A singleton has its one connected empty word at genus zero and no transposition word at positive genus. Negative genus is assigned count zero.

## A.1. Counts and normalization

Let `alpha=(alpha_1,...,alpha_c)` be the cycle lengths of a specified permutation on `n` labels. The indices enumerate actual cycles, including repeated lengths as distinct objects. Let `C_g(alpha)` denote the connected fixed-permutation word count and `w_g(alpha)=C_g(alpha)/r!`, where `r=n+c-2+2g`.

Put `a_k=k^k/(k-1)!` and `z_alpha=product_k k^(m_k) m_k!`. Goulden–Jackson's fixed-permutation convention gives `mu_g(alpha)=C_g(alpha)/z_alpha`; hence their generating function has coefficient `C_g(alpha)/(z_alpha r!)`. Vakil's distinguished-cycle convention is compatible with this conversion. Scalar counts are insensitive to a possible reversal of a source's composition convention: reversal of a word under a fixed convention inverts its product, and inversion preserves cycle type. The decoder below nevertheless uses the explicit rightmost-first convention throughout.

The genus-zero formula is

`w_0(alpha) = n^(c-3) product_i a_(alpha_i)`.

For positive genus define linear forms `Psi_j(u)=sum_k k^(j-1) a_k p_k u^k`, and let `s=x exp(Psi_0(s))`. The authorized genus-one and genus-two generating functions are `F_g=H_g(Psi(s))`, where

```
H_1 = (-log(1-Psi_1)-Psi_0)/24,
H_2 = [Q_3/(1-Psi_1)^3 + Q_4/(1-Psi_1)^4
       + Q_5/(1-Psi_1)^5]/5760,
Q_3 = 5Psi_4-12Psi_3+7Psi_2,
Q_4 = 29Psi_2 Psi_3-25Psi_2^2,
Q_5 = 28Psi_2^3.
```

These imported formulas are the only positive-genus enumeration dependency for A. No prescribed-support completion count is imported.

For clarity, every valid permutation on `n>=2` has a nonempty fiber for every `g>=0`: factor each cycle minimally; append doubled transpositions linking the cycle supports in a tree; then append `g` further doubled transpositions. This constructs a connected word of the required length and endpoint. Thus, on A's valid inputs, zero recognition consists precisely of rejecting positive-genus singleton fibers; invalid permutation encodings can be rejected separately.

## A.2. A finite expression suitable for conditional sums

This section derives, rather than assumes, the feature of the count formulas needed for a decoder.

Replace `p_k` by the sum of separate variables `y_i` for cycles of length `k`. Coefficient extraction in `y_1...y_c` supplies the missing `product_k m_k!`; multiplying by `product_i alpha_i` then converts the covering normalization to the fixed-permutation count. Let

`L_j = sum_i alpha_i^(j-1) a_(alpha_i) y_i`,

and let `D` act on expressions in the `L_j` by `D L_j=L_(j+1)`. Lagrange inversion gives, for `g=1,2`,

`w_g(alpha) = (product_i alpha_i)/n * [y_1...y_c] exp(n L_0) D H_g(L)`.

Indeed, `[x^n]H(Psi(s)) = (1/n)[u^(n-1)] H'(Psi(u)) exp(n Psi_0(u))`. Multiplying the derivative by `u` produces `D H`. The target squarefree monomial has weighted degree `sum alpha_i=n`, so its `u` coefficient is obtained by setting `u=1` after retaining those variables. This proves the displayed identity.

Crucially, `D H_g` has a constant-size list of terms

`kappa * product_(s=1)^t L_(j_s) / (1-L_1)^d`,

with `t<=4`, `j_s<=5`, and `d<=6`. A fully explicit list is:

| g | coefficient denominator | numerator coefficient | ordered numerator exponents | d |
|---|---:|---:|---|---:|
| 1 | 24 | 1 | (2) | 1 |
| 1 | 24 | -1 | (1) | 0 |
| 2 | 5760 | 5 | (5) | 3 |
| 2 | 5760 | -12 | (4) | 3 |
| 2 | 5760 | 7 | (3) | 3 |
| 2 | 5760 | 44 | (2,4) | 4 |
| 2 | 5760 | 29 | (3,3) | 4 |
| 2 | 5760 | -86 | (2,3) | 4 |
| 2 | 5760 | 21 | (2,2) | 4 |
| 2 | 5760 | 200 | (2,2,3) | 5 |
| 2 | 5760 | -100 | (2,2,2) | 5 |
| 2 | 5760 | 140 | (2,2,2,2) | 6 |

Here numerator slots are ordered even when their exponents coincide. Define `b_d(q)=binom(d+q-1,q)` for `d>=1`, and `b_0(q)=1` if `q=0`, zero otherwise. For each term, choose an injection of its `t` slots into distinct actual cycles; call the remaining cycle set `R`. The term's contribution to `w_g` is

```
(product_i a_(alpha_i))/n * kappa
  * product_s alpha_(i_s)^(j_s)
  * sum_(q=0)^|R| b_d(q) q! n^(|R|-q) e_q(alpha_R).
```

The elementary symmetric polynomial selects which unmarked cycles arise from the denominator, and its complement arises from the exponential. The factor `q!` comes from expanding `L_1^q`; the exponential's squarefree coefficient has no additional factorial. Summing all slot injections and all listed terms gives the exact count. Terms can have either sign; the algorithm never treats individual signed terms as probabilities.

The genus-zero formula fits the same computation with no marked slots, no denominator selections, scalar `n^-3`, and an independent factor `n` for every cycle. Positive-genus formulas use scalar `n^-1`. Singleton cases are overridden explicitly, so no zero-fiber ambiguity remains.

## A.3. Exact deletion decomposition

For a recursive input `(V,sigma,g)` with positive length, inspect every transposition `t=(a b)` and set `tau=sigma t`.

If `a,b` lie in different cycles of `sigma`, then `tau` has one fewer cycle. The prefix is necessarily connected and has genus `g`. To see necessity, if the deleted last edge disconnected the support, its endpoints would lie in different prefix components, and multiplication by that edge would merge two prefix cycles into a single endpoint cycle of `sigma`. That contradicts their belonging to distinct cycles of `sigma`.

If `a,b` lie in the same cycle of `sigma`, then `tau` has one additional cycle. The prefix is either connected with genus `g-1`, or has exactly two support components. Deleting one edge occurrence from a connected multigraph cannot create more than two components. In the disconnected case, the two new `tau` cycles containing `a` and `b` must belong to opposite components. Designate the child containing `a` as A and the child containing `b` as B, avoiding any factor-of-two ambiguity. Every other actual cycle of `tau` belongs wholly to one child.

For a partition of these cycles into `A,B`, write child degrees `n_A,n_B`, cycle counts `c_A,c_B`, and genera `g_A,g_B`. The length identity forces `g_A+g_B=g`. Conversely, any such partition, two connected child words, an order-preserving interleaving of them, and final edge `t` produce a connected parent word. The number of words for this precise branch is

```
binom(r-1,r_A) C_(g_A)(alpha_A) C_(g_B)(alpha_B)
   = (r-1)! w_(g_A)(alpha_A) w_(g_B)(alpha_B),
r_A = n_A+c_A-2+2g_A.
```

Every parent word has one final transposition and one such connected/disconnected decomposition. Consequently these are disjoint counting branches and their counts sum exactly to `C_g(alpha)`. Negative child genus, positive-genus singleton children, and any other zero counts are omitted. This is a direct combinatorial identity, not an assumption about a source's recursion.

## A.4. Polynomial computation of every needed subset mass

Fix a final edge, a child genus pair with sum at most two, and any prescribed child memberships for some cycles, including the two anchors. The required operation is the exact sum

`S = sum_(compatible A,B) w_(g_A)(alpha_A) w_(g_B)(alpha_B)`.

Here is an explicit polynomial algorithm. Loop over `n_A=1,...,n-1`, with `n_B=n-n_A`. Skip a positive-genus singleton child. Choose one term from each child's constant-size expression in A.2; for genus zero use its special one-term expression. Choose distinct cycle indices for all the marked slots of both terms. There are at most eight slots, so at most `n^8` injections, a deliberately loose bound uniform over all permitted genus pairs. Reject injections inconsistent with prescribed memberships.

The common factor `product_i a_(alpha_i)` is independent of the partition. Each marked cycle contributes its specified power of `alpha_i`, fixes its child, and contributes its length toward that child's degree. For every remaining cycle of length `a`, use the following four choices:

| choice | contribution to A degree | increment | weight |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| A exponential | a | none | n_A |
| A denominator | a | q_A by 1 | a |
| B exponential | 0 | none | n_B |
| B denominator | 0 | q_B by 1 | a |

Omit choices violating a prescribed child membership. A dynamic program over `(A degree,q_A,q_B)` accumulates their integer weights. Each coordinate is at most `n`, so there are `O(n^3)` states and `O(n^4)` elementary updates. At the target A degree, multiply each coefficient by `q_A! b_(d_A)(q_A) q_B! b_(d_B)(q_B)`, the two scalar factors, the marked-cycle weights, and the two signed term coefficients. Sum all choices of terms, injections, and child degree. This gives exactly `S` by the expansion proved in A.2.

In particular, there is no table indexed by a general subset of cycles, nor a list of all cycle partitions. Actual equal-length cycles remain distinct throughout the item-by-item DP. The only enumerated marked subsets have bounded cardinality. The child cycle counts do not need to be DP coordinates: the shuffle factorial cancellation in A.3 removes `r_A! r_B!` before the inventory is formed.

The same algorithm permits any previously forced membership decisions. Thus it supplies every conditional mass needed for sequentially choosing a cycle partition, including choices after observing an earlier assignment. These are exact restricted counts; no claim that unconditioned marginal probabilities survive conditioning is used.

## A.5. The explicit sampler, its law, and bit cost

The sampler is the following recursive procedure.

1. Validate the encoded permutation and handle singleton and zero cases. Otherwise evaluate the count using A.1–A.2.
2. Calculate the disjoint masses in A.3 for every possible last transposition, connected branch, or disconnected child genus pair. For a disconnected class sum its cycle-partition masses using A.4. Multiply the normalized sum by `(r-1)!` to obtain an integer count.
3. Choose a class with probability equal to its count divided by the parent count.
4. For a disconnected class, decide each remaining cycle's child in a fixed order. Compute both exact restricted masses by A.4 and choose in their ratio. A zero-mass choice is never selected. At the end recurse on the two specified child permutations, independently conditional on the chosen branch, and uniformly interleave their words. For a connected class recurse on `tau` at its specified child genus.
5. Append the selected last transposition.

Children retain their literal labels. Equivalently, one may relabel a child by increasing label order, run the same procedure, and invert that relabeling. Uniform interleaving is sampled by a uniform `r_A`-subset of the `r-1` positions, using exact binomial counts.

For exact discrete choices, draw a uniform integer below the positive integer total by binary rejection from `ceil(log2(total))` fair bits. The acceptance probability is greater than one half (and total one requires no bits). Compare the resulting integer to cumulative integer masses. All restricted branch masses are nonnegative integers because A.3 identifies them as actual word counts, even though their formula evaluation uses signed rational intermediates.

Correctness is induction on word length. In a connected branch, multiplying branch selection probability by the uniform recursive conditional probability gives `1/C_parent`. In a disconnected branch the count is `binom(r-1,r_A) C_A C_B`; independent uniform child words and uniform interleaving give the reciprocal of exactly that count. The cycle-membership probabilities telescope through the actual restricted masses, so their joint probability is precisely the intended partition weight. Every parent word has one decomposition, hence is output with probability `1/C_parent`.

For runtime, `r<=2n+2`. Every nonempty recursive call emits one transposition and reduces the total remaining child length by one; the recursion tree therefore has `r` nonempty nodes and at most `r+1` terminal calls. All genera stay in `{0,1,2}`. A mass query as described uses at most `O(n^13)` arithmetic operations: one child-degree loop, at most `n^8` slot injections, and an `O(n^4)` DP, times an absolute constant number of terms. Enumerating at most `n^2` last edges and at most `n` membership decisions remains polynomial at each of `O(n)` nodes.

All integer and rational intermediates have polynomial bit length. More precisely, each individual factor `alpha_i^(alpha_i)` or `n^c`, and products over all cycles, factorials up to `2n+2`, DP sums over at most `4^n` choices, and bounded-slot factors have `O(n log n)` bits. For fixed child degrees, the common rational denominator of a partition sum can be taken to divide a constant times `product_i (alpha_i-1)! n_A^3 n_B^3`; the bounded genus-dependent constants add only constant bits. Summing over child degrees requires at worst an additional denominator dividing `((n-1)!)^6`, which still has `O(n log n)` bits. There is no product over exponentially many independently chosen denominators. Count values themselves are bounded by `binom(n,2)^(2n+2)`.

Using elementary exact binary arithmetic, even a deliberately loose bound `C n^25` expected bit operations for `n>=2` covers these loops, rational arithmetic, fair-bit choices, and output. The exponent and constant are uniform over `g=0,1,2`. Memory is polynomial as well. The random rejection steps have constant expected attempts; the unbounded tail does not alter exactness or the expected-time assertion.

The output length is literal and has `O(n log n)` bits. This proves A's degree-polynomial expected bit model. The result is not polynomial in `log n`. Since the resulting law is exactly uniform, its TV error is zero and hence at most `2^-b` for every `b>=1`; no numerical approximation parameter is needed.

## B.1. Fixed-word normalization and exact component enumeration

Write `h_(g,v)` for DYZ's coefficients. Their convention is `h_(g,v)=H_(g,v)/(2g+2v-2)!`, with inverse-automorphism weights. Section 1.2 identifies the all-covering count multiplied by `v!` with ordered identity words and relates it to connected counts by the exponential formula. Restricting the same relabeling action to transitive tuples proves

`C(v,ell) = v! ell! h_(g,v)`, with `ell=2v-2+2g`.

Indeed, an orbit of transitive labeled tuples has `v!/|Aut|` elements; dividing the total labeled count by `v!` gives exactly its inverse-automorphism sum. This confirms the package's proposed normalization. Isolates contribute the single `(v,ell)=(1,0)` object. It would be incorrect to introduce any positive-genus singleton component.

For a collection of nontrivial component multiplicities `k_(v,g)`, let `s=sum v k_(v,g)` and `m=sum ell k_(v,g)`. Counting distinct vertex assignments, component words, and their interleavings gives exactly

`(N)_s m! product_(v,g) h_(g,v)^(k_(v,g))/k_(v,g)!`,

provided `s<=N`. The unused labels are isolates. This is an elementary labeled-component derivation of the exponential formula and keeps the exact bridge normalizer intact.

## B.2. An all-genus positive majorant

The primary recurrence and base values are

```
h_(g,v) = 2/[v^2(v-1)]
  sum_(a+b=v) sum_(g_1+g_2+l=g+1, l>=1)
  [a^(2l+1) b^2/(2l)!] h_(g_1,a) h_(g_2,b),
h_(0,v) = v^(v-3)/v!,
h_(0,1)=1, h_(g,1)=0 for g>0.
```

Here and below sums only use nonnegative genera. Define `R_g(v)=h_(g,v)/h_(0,v)` and

`p_v(a)=a^3 (v-a)^2 h_(0,a) h_(0,v-a) / [v^2(v-1)h_(0,v)]`.

The genus-zero instance of the recurrence proves that these positive numbers sum to one. With `R_v(z)=sum_g R_g(v) z^g`, the full recurrence becomes

`R_v(z)=sum_a p_v(a) R_a(z) R_(v-a)(z) sum_(k>=0) [2a^(2k)/(2k+2)!] z^k`.

Induction on `v` gives the coefficientwise bound

`R_v(z) <= (1-v^2 z)^(-(v-1))`.

The base `v=1` is `R_1=1`. For a split `a+b=v`, each child series is bounded by replacing its degree by `v` in its inductive majorant. The final series has coefficients at most `v^(2k)`, so is bounded by `(1-v^2 z)^-1`. The exponents sum to `(a-1)+(b-1)+1=v-1`; averaging with `p_v` preserves this coefficientwise inequality. In particular,

`h_(g,v) <= h_(0,v) v^(2g) binom(v+g-2,g)` for `v>=2,g>=0`.       (B-M)

This is an independent derivation from the positive scalar recurrence; no finite asymptotic or heuristic is used.

For `g<=2^j`, use `h_(0,v)<=exp(v)` and `binom(v+g-2,g)<=(v+g)^g` to obtain

`h_(g,v) <= exp(v) (v+g)^(3g) <= exp(4v) 2^(3jg)`.              (B-L)

For the last step, `v+g <= 2^j(1+v/2^j)` and `log(1+u)<=u`, while `g<=2^j`. This also covers `g=0` directly.

## B.3. A single fixed tilt bounds the event numerator

Fix the input subsequence and choose

`x=2^(-5j-6)`, `N=2^(8j)`, `m=2^(6j)`.

The cutoff is the original `L`, never recomputed for a component or descendant. Since `P=N+m+2<=2N` and `3^L<=P^4`, we have `L<=32j+4<=33j` for `j>=64`.

Let `S` be the sum of `h_(g,v)N^v x^(2v-2+2g)` over the allowed nontrivial components. Bound the union defining the event by adding the low-genus sum over all `v>=2` and the small-degree sum over all `g>=0`; counting the overlap twice is harmless for this upper bound.

For low genus, (B-L) gives, with `q=exp(4)2^(-2j-12)` and `rho=2^(-7j-12)`,

```
S_low <= x^-2 [q^2/(1-q)] [1/(1-rho)]
      = [exp(8)/4096] m / [(1-q)(1-rho)].
```

Both `q,rho<=1/32`. Since `exp(8)<3^8=6561`,

`S_low <= (6561/3844)m < (7/4)m`.

For `v<=L`, one has `v^2 x^2<=1/2`. For example `33j<=2^(j+6)` and hence `Lx<=2^(-4j)`. Summing (B-M) in genus at `x^2` gives

`sum_g h_(g,v)x^(2g) <= h_(0,v)(1-v^2x^2)^(-(v-1)) <= exp(2v)`.

The last inequality follows from `h_(0,v)<=exp(v)` and `(1-v^2x^2)^(-(v-1))<=2^(v-1)<=exp(v)`. Therefore, with `q_0=exp(2)2^(-2j-12)<=1/32`,

```
S_small <= x^-2 q_0^2/(1-q_0)
        <= [81/3968] m < m/4.
```

It follows that `S<=2m`. All generating functions evaluated here converge by the displayed geometric majorants.

Let `T=|Omega(N,m) intersect E_(G,L)|`. The exact component count of B.1 and `(N)_s<=N^s` give

`T <= m! [z^m] exp(sum_allowed h_(g,v)N^v z^ell) <= m! x^-m exp(S)`.

This only adds terms with total used labels above `N`; positivity justifies the bound. Using `m!<=m^m` and `2 log_2(e)<3`,

`log_2 T <= (11j+9)m`.                                        (B-N)

No conditional independence of components under the bridge is assumed: this is a direct exact count followed by a positive generating-function bound.

## B.4. An independent lower bound for the exact bridge denominator

Set `k=floor(m/(8j))`. For `j>=64`, `m/(16j)<=k<=m/(8j)`, `k>=2`, and `k<=N`. Restrict to transpositions of the first `k` labels. Let `A(pi)` count length `m/2` words with endpoint `pi` in `S_k`. Reversal gives the same count at `pi^-1`. Thus Cauchy–Schwarz gives

```
|Omega(N,m)| >= |Omega(k,m)|
              = sum_pi A(pi)^2
              >= binom(k,2)^m/k!.
```

Since `binom(k,2)>=k^2/4` and `log_2(k!)<=k log_2 k<=3m/4`,

`log_2 |Omega(N,m)| >= (12j-2log_2(j)-43/4)m`.                  (B-D)

The lower bound uses a fixed embedded subgroup; it requires neither a mixing theorem nor a representation-theoretic approximation to the normalizer.

Combining (B-N) and (B-D),

`log_2 U_(N,m)(E_(G,L)) <= [-j+2log_2(j)+79/4]m`.

For every integer `j>=64`, `j/2-2log_2(j)>=20`. Equality holds at 64 and the expression increases thereafter; for example its one-step increment is at least `1/2-4/j>0`. Therefore

`U_(N,m)(E_(G,L)) <= 2^(-(j/2+1/4)m) <= 2^(-1-jm/2)`,

because `m/4>=1`. This proves B with explicit slack. The hypothesis on fixed `G` is exactly what ensures `G<=2^j`, including `G=0`. Finally, any law `Q` supported on this event satisfies `TV(Q,U)>=Q(E)-U(E)=1-U(E)` by the event characterization of TV.
