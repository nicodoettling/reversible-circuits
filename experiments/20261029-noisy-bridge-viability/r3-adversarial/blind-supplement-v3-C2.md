## C2 identity and support assessment

The path product is the cycle (a_1 ... a_v) under rightmost-first composition. The ascending star product is its inverse (this is checked by following each a_i). Hence the emitted word has identity product. At m>=2, path edge intersections recover the ordered injection; for m=2 the shared middle vertex and ordered edges recover both endpoints. The entropy lower bound is consequently justified as in C1, though it supplies no uniformity guarantee.

Every Hurwitz move preserves the subgroup generated by the factors, hence the support exactly. Thus before noise the candidate always uses v=m+1 labels, regardless of s, including s=L^3. After k replacements, its support has size at most m+1+2k. The adversary counts labels touched by the complete observed word and accepts below this threshold.

## Independent reference bound for the declared sparse schedule

If the reference observed word has support at most m+1+2k, its original global bridge has support at most a=m+1+4k, because each replacement can have removed at most two old labels. For a<=N,

    P_reference[accept] <= binom(N,a) F_(2m)(a)/F_(2m)(N).

This is exactly B1.2, with no connected conditioning. Bound the numerator by binom(N,a)*binom(a,2)^(2m). For the denominator count only m vertex-disjoint edges, each appearing twice, in arbitrary order:

    F_(2m)(N) >= [(2m)!/(4^m m!)]*(N)_(2m),

valid for N>=2m. This subset count is complete for its chosen family, not asserted to exhaust the reference. Combining gives

    P_reference[accept] <= N^a a^(4m) m! / [a! (2m)! (N)_(2m)].

For N=m^6, k=floor(sqrt(m)), and m>=81, we have m<=a<=3m/2. Also (N)_(2m)>=N^(2m)/2 by product_(j=0)^(2m-1)(1-j/N)>=1-m(2m-1)/N>=1/2. Since (2m)!/m!>=m^m and a!>=(a/3)^a,

    P_reference[accept]
      <= 2 N^(a-2m) a^(4m-a) 3^a m^(-m)
      <= 2 [ (27/8)*3^(3/2) / m ]^m
      < 2 (18/m)^m.

The middle step uses N^(a-2m)<=m^(-3m), a^(4m-a)<=(3m/2)^(3m), and 3^a<=3^(3m/2). The elementary factorial inequality follows, for example, from sum_(j=1)^a log j >= integral_1^a log x dx, giving a!>=(a/e)^a and e<3.

Thus on the candidate's explicit schedule lambda=m>=81 the one-sample advantage is at least 1-2(18/lambda)^lambda. ## Boundary/regression points

- k=L: the candidate always satisfies the loose support threshold but the reference does too when it exceeds N. The asymptotic sparse schedule proof must not be extrapolated to this channel endpoint.
- N small or a>=N: the support upper bound becomes vacuous; S3L4/S4L4/S4L6 checks validate literal laws but do not establish the large-N attack bound.
- m=0,1 and N=2 are outside the candidate definition; the M0 empty/single-gate cases remain separate.
- Replacing a gate may equal its old value; all support inequalities remain valid, since they only use a maximum number of changed labels.
- The test counts gate-word support, not endpoint support. Endpoint support<=4k is common to both worlds and is not used as a distinguishing event.
