## Context

We are studying cryptographic properties of random reversible circuits on `n` wires with `m` gates.
The primary object of interest is a sampler for circuits that compute the identity permutation.

The user is interested in a cryptographic setting, so pseudorandom identity-circuit sampling should eventually be grounded in a computational hardness assumption rather than purely statistical mixing.

## Reversible-Circuit Formulation

Fix a reversible gate library `S_n` on `n` wires and let `G_n = <S_n>` be the permutation group generated on `{0,1}^n`.

A length-`m` circuit is a word

```text
g_m ... g_1,   g_i in S_n.
```

It computes the identity iff the product is the identity in `G_n`.
Thus identity circuits are exactly length-`m` closed walks at the identity in the Cayley graph `Cay(G_n, S_n)`.

Exact uniform identity-circuit sampling is therefore a random-walk bridge problem on a huge Cayley graph.
A random-walk bridge is an ordinary random walk conditioned on its endpoint.
Here, starting from `e`, a length-`m` bridge from `e` back to `e` is exactly a length-`m` loop in the Cayley graph, hence exactly a circuit word that evaluates to identity.

The exact bridge transition from current state `x` with `r` steps remaining is

```text
Pr[next gate = s] proportional to
#{length-(r-1) completions from xs back to e}.
```

This is exact but computationally infeasible in general because it requires endpoint-hitting counts in `G_n`.

Naive rejection from random circuits accepts with probability `Pr[X_m = e]`.
After mixing, this is about `1/|G_n|`, which is astronomically small.
For `G_n = A_{2^n}`, `log_2 |G_n| ~= n 2^n`.

## Presentations and Relations

If a group is represented as a free group modulo relations, the normal subgroup of relations depends on the chosen generators.
For the standard adjacent-transposition generators of the symmetric group,

```text
s_i = (i i+1),   i = 1,...,n-1,
```

the Coxeter presentation is

```text
S_n = < s_1,...,s_{n-1}
      | s_i^2 = 1,
        (s_i s_j)^2 = 1       for |i-j| > 1,
        (s_i s_{i+1})^3 = 1   for i = 1,...,n-2 >.
```

Equivalently, the kernel `N` of the natural map from the free group `F = <s_1,...,s_{n-1}>` to `S_n` is normally generated by

```text
s_i^2 = 1
s_i s_j = s_j s_i                              for |i-j| > 1
s_i s_{i+1} s_i = s_{i+1} s_i s_{i+1}          for i = 1,...,n-2.
```

These are involution, distant-commutation, and braid/Yang-Baxter relations.

If all transpositions `(ij)` are used as generators instead, a natural presentation uses

```text
(ij)^2 = 1
(ij)(kl) = (kl)(ij)              when {i,j} and {k,l} are disjoint
(ij)(jk) = (ik)(ij)              for distinct i,j,k.
```

For reversible circuits, the analogue is: choose a gate library, form the free group on gate symbols, and let `N` be the normal subgroup of all circuit identities.
Finding a small normal generating set for `N` is a finite-presentation problem for the generated permutation group and depends heavily on the gate library.

## Gate-Set Caveats

Gate choice matters.

The all-transpositions model is useful because it has explicit conditional
block samplers and a rich Hurwitz/support-component structure.  It should not
be treated as the only plausible model.  The long polynomial-mixing run left
open the possibility that the fixed small-block `{3,4,5}` transposition
heat-bath chain has hidden slow modes, especially in sparse or near-minimal
regimes and inside full-support fibers.

A serious alternative is to study bounded-size expander generating sets for
`S_N` or `A_N`, such as Kassabov-style generators.  In that benchmark model the
unconditioned Cayley walk has a uniform spectral gap, so the base walk mixes in
`O(log |G|)` steps up to constants.  The identity-word target is then the
uniform length-`m` loop law on an expander Cayley graph:

```text
Omega_S(N,m) = { (g_1,...,g_m) in S^m : g_1 ... g_m = id }.
```

This does not automatically solve bridge sampling: endpoint conditioning can
still create long-range dependence, and a local bridge heat-bath chain still
needs a mixing proof.  But it changes the technical problem from Hurwitz
weighted-component expansion to bridge dynamics on bounded-degree expanders.
This is likely a cleaner benchmark if the all-transpositions polynomial-mixing
conjecture fails.

Main caveat: Kassabov generators are global permutations of `N` points.  If
`N = 2^n` is the state space of an `n`-wire reversible circuit, these
generators are not automatically small local reversible gates.  They are best
viewed as an abstract symmetric-group benchmark unless one supplies efficient
reversible-circuit implementations.  For cryptographic reversible circuits,
compare this direction with implementable local gate families from the random
reversible-circuit literature, such as random local reversible gates or simple
permutations.

Pure positive-control Toffoli gates are not a good cryptographic mixing library by themselves:

- They fix `0^n`.
- They fix every Hamming-weight-1 string.
- The generated permutation family is much smaller than all reversible functions.

For local gates acting on fewer than all `n` wires, each such embedded local gate is often an even permutation of the full `2^n` state space once there is at least one untouched wire.
Thus common local reversible libraries typically generate a subgroup of `A_{2^n}`, not all of `S_{2^n}`.

The pseudorandomness literature usually uses random reversible 3-bit gates or "simple permutations", not just positive Toffoli gates.
If the goal is cryptographic pseudorandomness, use a gate family already studied for approximate `k`-wise independence.

## Known Pseudorandomness Results

Most known results concern unconditioned random reversible circuits, not circuits conditioned to compute identity.

Known direction:

- Random reversible circuits can approximate `k`-wise independent permutations under suitable gate libraries and lengths/depths.
- Recent work improves gate/depth bounds using log-Sobolev and architecture-specific analyses.

Important caveat:

Conditioning the entire circuit to compute the identity destroys function-level pseudorandomness: the induced permutation is known.
The cryptographic object must therefore be the circuit description, hidden relations, subcircuits, prefixes, or the difficulty of distinguishing generated identity words from some target identity-word distribution.

## Identity-Circuit Samplers

Easy samplers exist but are structurally biased:

- Sample `C` and output `C C^{-1}`.
- Insert adjacent cancellations `g g^{-1}` or, for involutory gates, `g g`.
- Output commutators `A B A^{-1} B^{-1}`.
- Use known reversible-circuit templates/relations.

These are useful null-circuit generators, but they are not uniform closed-walk samplers and are likely distinguishable from random identity words by local or global circuit-description statistics.

Research problem:

```text
Construct high-entropy, efficiently samplable identity circuits whose descriptions are computationally indistinguishable from a suitable target distribution over identity circuits.
```

This seems different from just sampling random reversible functions.

## Rank-Recursive Transposition Sampler Direction

A separate candidate direction applies specifically to the all-transpositions
model.  Generalize the recursive interface to endpoint fibers

```text
Omega(N,L;g) = { (t_1,...,t_L) : t_i transpositions in S_N,
                                  t_1 ... t_L = g }.
```

The top-level identity sampler is still `Sample(N,L,e)`.  Arbitrary endpoints
are required internally when splitting `S_N` along the point stabilizer
`S_(N-1)`.  If

```text
C_N = sum_(t transposition in S_N) t,
J_N = sum_(i<N) (i N),
```

then `C_N=C_(N-1)+J_N` and the two summands commute.  Stable-sorting old and
star transpositions gives the positive exact disintegration

```text
[g] C_N^L
 = sum_k binom(L,k) sum_(h in S_(N-1))
     [h] C_(N-1)^(L-k) [h^(-1)g] J_N^k.
```

The September 2026 rank-recursive goal run resolved these primitives in the
dense fixed-length regime.  Its current theorem candidate gives, for every
sufficiently large `N` and every feasible endpoint `g`, a polynomial-time
sampler on `Omega(N,2N^3;g)` whose output law is within

```text
3N exp(-N/12288)
```

of uniform in total variation.  In particular, `g=e` gives statistically
close-to-uniform identity bridges.  The conservative exact-arithmetic runtime
bound recorded by the run is `O(N^4 L^3)`, hence `O(N^13)` at `L=2N^3`.

The proof combines the exact stable-sort disintegration above with an exact
prescribed-endpoint sampler for star factorizations, a pointwise smoothing
bound for the star walk, and a retained-length recursion that prevents the
lower-rank instances from becoming short.  Complete transposition support is
then a high-probability property of the uniform target: its failure probability
at `L=2N^3` is at most `exp(-2N)` in the stated parameter range.

This is a fixed-negligible-error theorem candidate, not an exact sampler or a
sampler with a user-specified accuracy parameter.  It rests on agent-produced
proofs and internal finite audits and therefore still requires line-by-line
human review before being treated as established.  The adjacent-pair lift is
also not itself exact: it occupies a restricted branch, and neither global
conjugation nor subsequent Hurwitz moves corrects that branch bias in general.

The derivation and run materials are in
`notes/rank-recursive-identity-bridge-sampling.md` and
`experiments/20260901-rank-recursive-identity-bridge-goal/`.  The streamlined
proof is in the manuscript.  This result supplies a separate dense-regime
sampler and does not prove polynomial mixing of the local heat-bath chain in
`GOAL_SPEC.md`.

## Expected-Length Relaxation

The fixed-`m` constraint is not essential for the user's cryptographic goal.
It would be acceptable to sample identity circuits with about `m` gates in expectation.

This relaxation changes the sampling picture in a useful way:

- Fixed length has a canonical target: uniform length-`m` identity words, equivalently length-`m` random-walk bridges.
- Expected length does not have a canonical uniform target, because there are infinitely many identity words.
- A natural maximum-entropy replacement is a Boltzmann/Gibbs distribution

```text
Pr[w] proportional to lambda^{|w|} * 1[eval(w) = id],
```

with `lambda` chosen so that `E[|w|] = m`.

Exact Boltzmann sampling is still hard in general.
The bridge completion counts are replaced by Green-function-style counts over all possible remaining lengths.
So the exact-sampling obstacle does not disappear.

However, expected length makes relator-based samplers much more natural.
Given a presentation

```text
G = < generators | relators R >,
```

every word in the normal closure of `R` evaluates to identity.
One can efficiently sample identity words by choosing random relators and random conjugating words:

```text
sample K
for j = 1..K:
    sample relator r_j in R
    sample random word c_j
    append c_j r_j c_j^{-1}
```

The expected length is tunable:

```text
E[|w|] ~= E[K] * (2 E[|c|] + E[|r|]),
```

up to cancellations.

Thus the relaxation changes the conclusion from "efficient generation is hard" to:

```text
Efficiently generating identity circuits is easy.
Efficiently generating identity circuits that look like a high-entropy natural target distribution is still hard.
```

The cryptographic bottleneck becomes hiding the relator-product construction.
This likely requires a randomized equivalent-circuit compiler, a relator-mixing assumption, or an explicitly defined pseudorandom target distribution that the sampler is designed to match.

## What LSN/sympLPN Contributes

The local paper introduces Learning Stabilizers with Noise (LSN), motivated as a quantum analogue of LPN.
The key classical working assumption is `sympLPN`.

From the paper:

- LPN decision form: distinguish `(A, Ax + e)` from `(A, u)`, where `A` is random over `F_2`, `x` is secret, and `e` is Bernoulli noise.
- sympLPN decision form: distinguish `(A, Ax + e)` from `(A, u)`, where `A in F_2^{2n x k}` is random full-rank isotropic, and `e` is depolarizing noise in symplectic representation.
- The isotropic structure is the key difference from ordinary LPN.
- The paper builds PKE/OT/OWF-style primitives from sympLPN/LSN in appropriate noise regimes.

Useful analogy:

```text
random structured code + noisy codeword + indistinguishability/search hardness
```

This suggests looking for a nonabelian or symmetric-group analogue of noisy random-code decoding.

## Candidate Symmetric-Group Assumption

A direct "replace `F_2^n` by `S_N`" version of LPN is not clean because `S_N` is nonabelian and has many visible invariants.
The more plausible analogue is noisy decoding of random permutation codes or random word maps into `S_N`/`A_N`.

Candidate decision assumption:

Let `G` be `S_N`, `A_N`, or the actual reversible-circuit group generated by the gate library.
Sample public permutations/circuits `A_1, ..., A_k`.
Sample secret `x in {0,1}^k`.
Define

```text
C_A(x) = A_1^{x_1} A_2^{x_2} ... A_k^{x_k}.
```

Sample short noise `E` near identity, e.g. a short random walk in the same local gate library.
Given `(A, Z)`, distinguish

```text
Z = C_A(x) E
```

from

```text
Z = U,   U uniform in G.
```

This is a "symmetric-group LPN" or "noisy random permutation-code" assumption.

Better balanced variant:

Use pairs of public blocks

```text
C_A(x) = A_{1,x_1} A_{2,x_2} ... A_{k,x_k},
```

where each `A_{i,b}` is near-uniform in `G`.
This avoids leaking secret Hamming weight and ensures every secret uses the same number of factors.

Search version:

- Recover `x`, or
- recover `C_A(x)`, or
- find a nearby codeword in the random permutation code.

## Plausibility Issues and Likely Attacks

A symmetric-group assumption must survive at least the following attacks.

1. Sign

If `G = S_N`, sign is a one-bit homomorphism and may distinguish structured/noisy samples.
Prefer `A_N` or ensure the noise and code distribution make sign uniform/irrelevant.

2. Cycle type

Cycle structure is a strong invariant of permutations.
Short noise near identity may not sufficiently hide the cycle type of `C_A(x)`.
Need empirical and theoretical tests of cycle statistics.

3. Support size and fixed points

Sparse local gate noise may preserve many points or create distinguishable support/fixed-point profiles.
For reversible circuits on `N = 2^n` points, local gates often have highly structured supports.

4. Low-dimensional representations

The standard representation of `S_N` and other irreducible representations may reveal biases.
Fourier/character tests should be considered.

5. Product-recovery / subset-product attacks

The structured sample is a noisy product of selected public permutations.
Meet-in-the-middle, birthday, and representation-based attacks may recover `x` for some parameter regimes.

6. Permutation-group algorithms

Random permutations usually generate `S_N` or `A_N`.
Subgroup formulations can collapse unless the "code" is a subset/list-code rather than a subgroup.

7. Representation of public elements

If public elements and samples are given as explicit products/circuit concatenations, the secret may be syntactically visible.
The assumption needs a representation model:

- full permutation table,
- black-box group oracle,
- randomized compiled reversible circuit,
- or another canonical hiding representation.

For reversible circuits, this is central: a cryptographic identity-circuit sampler must hide the word-level construction.

## Relation to Identity-Circuit Sampling

The symmetric-group noisy-code assumption alone gives pseudorandom noisy group elements, not identity circuits.
To get pseudorandom identity circuits, one plausible architecture is:

```text
1. Build a structured relation or noisy-code instance in the reversible group.
2. Convert it into a word known to evaluate to identity.
3. Apply a randomized equivalent-circuit compiler / relation-mixing process.
4. Assume the resulting identity word is computationally indistinguishable from a target identity-word distribution.
```

This likely needs an additional "relator-mixing" assumption:

```text
Given two equivalent reversible circuits, it is hard to distinguish the output of the randomized compiler from a random word in that equivalence class, or from random identity words at comparable length.
```

This is currently more speculative than sympLPN.

## Suggested Next Research Tasks

1. Define the exact group model.

Choose one:

- `G = A_{2^n}` abstractly,
- `G` generated by random 3-bit reversible gates,
- `G` generated by width-2 simple permutations,
- `G` generated by a practical NCT/Toffoli-like reversible library.

2. Define the sample representation.

This affects security heavily:

- full permutation table is enormous but clean,
- circuit representation is practical but leaks factorization unless compiled/scrambled,
- black-box group representation may be mathematically clean but less directly implementable.

3. Define noise.

Candidate noises:

- short random walk in the same gate library,
- random sparse transpositions/3-cycles if working abstractly in `A_N`,
- local reversible noise with support calibrated to hide cycle/support statistics.

4. Benchmark distinguishers.

Implement tests for:

- sign,
- cycle type,
- fixed-point count,
- support size,
- displacement/Hamming distance,
- character values for low-dimensional representations,
- word-level statistics if circuit descriptions are used.

5. Study exact small-`n` identity sampling.

For small `n`, enumerate `G_n` and do dynamic programming over the Cayley graph to sample exact random-walk bridges.
Use this as a reference distribution for testing biased identity samplers.

6. Study expected-length identity samplers.

Define a target length distribution, e.g. Poisson, geometric, or Boltzmann/Gibbs.
Compare simple products of conjugated relators against this target using small-`n` exact distributions where possible.

7. Study relator MCMC.

Build an MCMC chain on identity words using local reversible-circuit relations/templates.
Empirically test whether it mixes against small-`n` exact bridge distributions.

8. Formulate a formal assumption.

Start with a decision assumption:

```text
Decision SG-LPN/Gate-LPN:
Distinguish (A, C_A(x)E) from (A, U).
```

Then formulate the identity-circuit assumption:

```text
Decision Null-Circuit PRG:
Distinguish sampler output from the chosen target distribution over identity circuits.
```

The second assumption may be reducible to the first only if there is a clean compiler/scrambler theorem.

## Current Assessment

It is plausible to formulate a symmetric-group analogue of LPN/LSN, but the safe formulation is not a subgroup-learning problem.
It should be a noisy random permutation-code decoding problem with carefully chosen group, representation, and noise.

For the original identity-circuit goal, one likely needs two layers:

```text
noisy permutation-code hardness
+ circuit-equivalence / relator-mixing hardness
```

Relaxing exact length to expected length makes efficient construction easier because products of conjugated relators always evaluate to identity and have tunable expected length.
It does not by itself give a cryptographically pseudorandom sampler.

The first layer may be defensible as a new assumption after attack analysis.
The second layer is the more novel and less understood part.
