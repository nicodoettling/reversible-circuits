\section{Hurwitz Walks and Their Obstruction}

For a factorization $(g_1,\ldots,g_m)$ in a group, a right Hurwitz move at
position $i$ replaces
\[
  (g_i,g_{i+1})
  \mapsto
  (g_{i+1}, g_{i+1}^{-1}g_i g_{i+1}).
\]
The inverse move replaces
\[
  (g_i,g_{i+1})
  \mapsto
  (g_i g_{i+1}g_i^{-1}, g_i).
\]
For transpositions, conjugates are transpositions, so Hurwitz moves preserve
the generator set, word length, and total product.

Hurwitz moves are useful local scramblers, but they are orbit samplers rather
than global samplers.  The experiments track the support graph of a word: its
vertices are the points touched by at least one transposition and its edges are
the transpositions appearing in the word, with multiplicity.  A weighted
support-component invariant records the connected components of this support
graph together with the number of factors supported in each component.  Pure
Hurwitz moves preserve this invariant in the tested cases, and the exact
Hurwitz components coincide with invariant classes in the small references.

\begin{proposition}[Component obstruction]
Suppose a Markov chain on $\Omega(N,m)$ is confined to a Hurwitz component
$C\subsetneq \Omega(N,m)$.  Then, regardless of its mixing behavior inside
$C$, its total variation distance from the global uniform law on
$\Omega(N,m)$ is at least
\[
  1-\frac{|C|}{|\Omega(N,m)|}.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The chain assigns probability $0$ to $\Omega(N,m)\setminus C$, while the
uniform distribution assigns mass
$1-|C|/|\Omega(N,m)|$ to that complement.
\end{proof}

This table is the first key negative result: pure Hurwitz random walks cannot
sample the global fixed-length bridge distribution except in instances where
the relevant Hurwitz orbit already has full mass.
