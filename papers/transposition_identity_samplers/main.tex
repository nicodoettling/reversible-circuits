\begin{abstract}
We study the following finite sampling problem.  Let $T_N$ be the set of
transpositions in $S_N$, and let
\[
  \Omega(N,m)
  =
  \{(t_1,\ldots,t_m)\in T_N^m : t_1t_2\cdots t_m=\id\}.
\]
The target distribution is the uniform distribution on $\Omega(N,m)$.
This is the random-walk bridge distribution for the random-transposition
Cayley graph, conditioned to return to the identity at time $m$.

The completed experiments show that pure Hurwitz random walks are not global
samplers: they preserve a weighted support-component invariant and split even
small instances into many components.  The most promising sampler found so far
is a fixed-length block heat-bath chain: choose a contiguous block, keep its
product fixed, and resample exactly from all transposition blocks with that
product.  Exact block heat-bath is reversible for the uniform law on
$\Omega(N,m)$, and small exact cases show connected transition graphs and
positive spectral gaps.  A later run removed the main practical bottleneck by
constructing direct exact conditional samplers for block sizes $4$ and $5$ and
integrating them into an exact $\{3,4,5\}$ block heat-bath kernel.  The main
frontier is now theoretical: formalizing the Hurwitz weighted-support
classification in the project convention and proving, or refuting, polynomial
mixing for the exact local heat-bath chain.

This note consolidates the completed findings available through the direct
conditional-sampler run, the subsequent theory-frontier run, the polynomial
upper-bound attempt, and the later polynomial-mixing swarm.  The strongest
current result is conditional: polynomial mixing follows from precise
quantitative projection and fiber estimates, but no unconditional polynomial
mixing theorem is claimed.  A graph-theoretic reformulation identifies the
main object as a local-rewrite graph on ordered edge sequences of the complete
graph whose associated token-swapping schedule is the identity.  Because the
small-block transposition chain may have hidden slow modes, we also isolate
bounded-degree expander generator sets, including Kassabov-style generators,
as a separate benchmark direction for identity-bridge sampling.
\end{abstract}
