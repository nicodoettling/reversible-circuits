\begin{abstract}
We study the following finite sampling problem.  Let $T_N$ be the set of
transpositions in $S_N$, and let
\[
  \Omega(N,m)
  =
  \{(t_1,\ldots,t_m)\in T_N^m : t_1t_2\cdots t_m=\id\}.
\]
The target distribution is the uniform distribution on $\Omega(N,m)$.
This is the random-walk bridge distribution for the random-transposition
Cayley graph, conditioned to return to the identity at time $m$.

Our main result is a rank-recursive sampler for the dense schedule $m=2N^3$.
For every sufficiently large $N$, it always returns a length-$2N^3$
transposition word with product identity, runs in expected time polynomial in
$N$ and $m$, and has total-variation distance at most
\[
  3N\exp(-N/12288)
\]
from the uniform bridge law.  The proof combines an exact stable-sort
disintegration along $S_{N-1}\subset S_N$, an exact polynomial-time sampler
for prescribed-endpoint star factorizations, parity-aware pointwise smoothing,
and a retained-length recursion whose errors telescope over the ranks.  At the
same schedule, every transposition occurs with probability at least
$1-\exp(-2N)$ under the uniform bridge.  The approximation error is negligible
but fixed by $N$ and $m$; arbitrary requested accuracy remains open.

We also retain the local-dynamics picture.  Pure Hurwitz walks are not global
samplers because they preserve weighted support-component data.  Exact
$\{3,4,5\}$ block heat-bath updates are reversible for the uniform law and
pass small exact tests, but no unconditional polynomial mixing theorem for
that local chain is known.  Thus rank recursion supplies the current
polynomial-time sampling result, while local heat-bath mixing and bounded-degree
expander gate sets remain distinct structural questions.
\end{abstract}
