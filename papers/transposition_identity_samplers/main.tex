\begin{abstract}
We study the following finite sampling problem.  Let $T_N$ be the set of
transpositions in $S_N$, and let
\[
  \Omega(N,m)
  =
  \{(t_1,\ldots,t_m)\in T_N^m : t_1t_2\cdots t_m=\id\}.
\]
The target distribution is the uniform distribution on $\Omega(N,m)$.
This is the random-walk bridge distribution for the random-transposition
Cayley graph, conditioned to return to the identity at time $m$.

Our main result is a rank-recursive sampler for the dense schedule $m=2N^3$.
For every sufficiently large $N$, it always returns a length-$2N^3$
transposition word with product identity, runs in expected time polynomial in
$N$ and $m$, and has total-variation distance at most
\[
  3N\exp(-N/12288)
\]
from the uniform bridge law.  The proof combines an exact stable-sort
disintegration along $S_{N-1}\subset S_N$, an exact polynomial-time sampler
for prescribed-endpoint star factorizations, parity-aware pointwise smoothing,
and a retained-length recursion whose errors telescope over the ranks.  At the
same schedule, every transposition occurs with probability at least
$1-\exp(-2N)$ under the uniform bridge.  The approximation error is negligible
but fixed by $N$ and $m$; arbitrary requested accuracy remains open.

We also retain the local-dynamics picture.  Pure Hurwitz walks are not global
samplers because they preserve weighted support-component data.  Exact
$\{3,4,5\}$ block heat-bath updates are reversible for the uniform law.
However, a balanced support-component bottleneck rules out a mixing bound
polynomial in $N,m$ over the entire parameter range: for every sufficiently
large even $m$, some $N$ between constant multiples of
$m^{3/2}/(\log m)^2$ and $m^{3/2}$ has mixing time at least
$\exp(\Omega(m/(\log m)^2))$.  The obstruction applies to every
uniform-stationary walk changing a bounded number of gates per step.
It also gives superpolynomial slowdown for update budgets
$b=o(m/(\log m)^3)$ along the same family.  A separately specified
ideal exact heat bath with windows of order $N\log N$ mixes rapidly
at every feasible length, with conditional-sampling cost left separate.
An all-endpoint conductance bound at length $\Theta(N\log N)$ would
imply rapid pair-chain mixing at every longer polynomial length.
For the adjacent pair chain at $m\ge CN\log N$, by contrast, the minimum
conductance over support-profile cuts is $\Theta(N^{-3})$; full-chain
mixing there remains unresolved.  At quadratic length, well-separated
prefix cuts also have conductance at least $cN^{-3}$, and the full
conductance question reduces to cuts invariant under relabeling.
These local-walk results leave direct
sampling and other generator models as distinct questions.
\end{abstract}
